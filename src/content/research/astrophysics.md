We study extreme astrophysical systems using numerical simulations, statistical topology, and machine learning. In gravitational-wave astrophysics, our work focuses on core-collapse supernovae, using entropy measures and neural networks to classify signals, constrain the nuclear equation of state, and estimate parameter horizons for current and next-generation detectors.

In stellar dynamics, we analyze the spatial structure of Gaia open clusters through fractal dimensions, $k$-nearest neighbor graphs, and network centrality metrics to quantify mass segregation, alongside direct $N$-body modeling of clusters such as NGC 2516 and chaos in three-body systems. We also develop predictive and convolutional models to classify variable stars, reconstruct gamma-ray burst light curves, and track photometric variability in Be/X-ray binaries.
